1 Executive Summary & Operational Scope

Tool-6 (HVAC Duct Loss Calculator & Critical Path Estimator) provides automated aerodynamic static pressure loss calculations, Total Equivalent Length (TEL), and Total Dynamic Loss (TDL) modeling for complex commercial HVAC supply and return air duct systems. The software is engineered to assist mechanical designers and consulting engineers in determining equipment External Static Pressure (ESP) requirements, identifying aerodynamically demanding critical paths, and validating duct geometry against verified engineering benchmarks.

3 Fundamental Aerodynamic Equations

3.1 Thermodynamic Air Properties

Calculations assume standard atmospheric air at sea level unless otherwise specified:

Patm = 29.921 in. Hg
Tstd = 70.0 °F (529.67 R)
ρ = 0.0750 lbm/ft³
ν = 1.63 × 10⁻⁴ ft²/s

3.2 Cross-Sectional Area ($A$) and Velocity ($V$)

Duct cross-sectional area $A$ (in square feet) is computed based on nominal dimensions:

Round Ducts (Diameter $D$ in.) $$A = \frac{\pi \cdot D^2}{4 \cdot 144} = \frac{\pi \cdot D^2}{576} \quad [\text{ft}^2]$$
Rectangular Ducts ($W \times H$ in.) $$A = \frac{W \cdot H}{144} \quad [\text{ft}^2]$$

The average duct velocity $V$ (feet per minute) for volumetric flow rate $Q$ (CFM) is:

$$V = \frac{Q}{A} \quad [\text{FPM}]$$

3.3 Velocity Pressure ($P_v$)

Velocity pressure represents kinetic energy per unit volume of the air stream, expressed in inches of water gauge ($\text{in. w.g.}$):

$$P_v = \rho \cdot \frac{(V / 60)^2}{2 \cdot g_c \cdot \rho_{w}} = \left(\frac{V}{4005}\right)^2 \quad [\text{in. w.g.}]$$

3.4 Huebscher Circular Equivalent Diameter ($D_e$)

In rectangular ducts, fluid boundary layers become non-axisymmetric due to corner turbulence. Tool-6 applies the empirical Huebscher Formula (ASHRAE Fundamentals Ch. 21, Eq. 25) to calculate the circular equivalent diameter $D_e$ having identical friction rate at equivalent CFM:

$$D_e = 1.30 \cdot \frac{(W \cdot H)^{0.625}}{(W + H)^{0.25}} \quad [\text{inches}]$$

4 Straight Duct Friction Loss Modeling

Straight duct head loss is governed by the Darcy-Weisbach formulation $\Delta P_f = f \cdot (L/D_h) \cdot P_v$. For standard galvanized steel HVAC ductwork with an absolute roughness of $\epsilon = 0.0003\text{ ft}$ ($0.09\text{ mm}$), Tool-6 evaluates the standard ASHRAE/SMACNA power-law relationship:

$$DFL = \frac{0.10913 \cdot Q^{1.90}}{D_e^{5.02}} \quad \left[\frac{\text{in. w.g.}}{100\text{ ft}}\right]$$

For a duct segment of physical length $L$ (ft), the total static pressure drop is:

$$\Delta P_{\text{straight}} = \left(\frac{L}{100}\right) \cdot DFL \quad [\text{in. w.g.}]$$

Note: This power-law formulation correlates with implicit Colebrook-White solutions to within ±1.2% across normal commercial HVAC design velocities (600 to 3,000 FPM).

5 Fitting Loss Formulation & The Unified Bridge Constant ($K_{ref}$)

A historic dilemma in HVAC engineering is reconciling the SMACNA Local Loss Coefficient ($C_o$) method with the Loren Cook Equivalent Length ($L_{eq}$) method:

SMACNA / ASHRAE Method: $$\Delta P = C_o \cdot P_v$$

Loss scales with dynamic velocity pressure.

Loren Cook Catalog Method: $$\Delta P = L_{eq} \cdot 0.001 \cdot \left(\frac{V}{1350}\right)^2$$

Loss tabulated at 1350 FPM and 0.10" w.g./100 ft baseline.

Mathematical Unification Proof

Equating the static loss of a fitting under baseline reference conditions ($V_{ref} = 1350\text{ FPM}$, $DFL_{ref} = 0.10\text{" w.g.}/100'$):

$$P_{v, ref} = \left(\frac{1350}{4005}\right)^2 \approx 0.11362206\text{ in. w.g.}$$ $$C_o \cdot P_{v, ref} = \left(\frac{L_{eq}}{100}\right) \cdot DFL_{ref} \implies C_o \cdot 0.11362206 = L_{eq} \cdot 0.001$$ $$\frac{C_o}{L_{eq}} = \frac{0.001}{0.11362206} = \frac{1}{113.62706}$$

We define the Unified Cook-SMACNA Reference Constant ($K_{ref}$):

$$K_{ref} = 100 \cdot \frac{\left(\frac{1350}{4005}\right)^2}{0.10} \approx \mathbf{113.62706\text{ ft}}$$

Tool-6 utilizes this exact bridge constant to evaluate every fitting dynamic loss:

$$C_o = \frac{L_{eq}}{113.627}$$ $$\Delta P_{\text{fitting}} = C_o \cdot P_v = \left(\frac{L_{eq}}{113.627}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{V}{4005}\right)^2 \quad [\text{in. w.g.}]$$

This identity guarantees that at any velocity $V$, the loss scales rigorously with $V^2$, perfectly honoring physical law and reconciling SMACNA $C_o$ with Loren Cook $L_{eq}$.

6 Continuous Interpolation & Surface Engine

Traditional calculators perform nearest-neighbor table lookups, introducing large artificial discontinuities when duct dimensions change by small increments. Tool-6 employs mathematical interpolation algorithms:

1D Piecewise Linear Interpolation:

Used for 1-variable curves (e.g. Elbow R/D, Aspect H/W, Velocity Ratio Vb/Vt):

$$y(x) = y_i + \frac{x - x_i}{x_{i+1} - x_i} \cdot (y_{i+1} - y_i)$$
2D Bilinear Surface Interpolation:

Used for complex tees (e.g. 5a Rect Branch across Vb/Vt and Qb/Qt):

$$y(u, v) = y_j(v) + \frac{u - u_j}{u_{j+1} - u_j} \cdot [y_{j+1}(v) - y_j(v)]$$

8 Continuous Aerodynamic Transition Engine (9a–9d, 10a–10c)

Duct transitions undergo both area expansion/contraction loss and cross-sectional transformation loss. Tool-6 defines the true continuous area ratio:

$$R = \frac{\max(A_1, A_2)}{\min(A_1, A_2)} \ge 1.0$$

Physical Boundary Baseline at $R = 1.0$ (Equal Area):

Same-Shape Transitions (9a, 9b, 10a, 10b, 10c):

Zero shape transformation and zero expansion/contraction:

Leq(1.0) = 0.0' EL
Shape Changes (9c Round→Rect, 9d Rect→Round):

Pure boundary transformation vortex form loss ($C_o \approx 0.05 \text{ to } 0.16$):

≤30°: 6' | 45°: 8' | 60°: 10' | 90°: 14' | ≥120°: 18' EL

The 9d Rect-to-Round Resolution: For a $20" \times 15"$ rectangular duct ($A_1 = 300\text{ in}^2$) transitioning to a $\varnothing 20"$ round duct ($A_2 = 314.16\text{ in}^2$) at $30^\circ$:

Area Ratio: R = 314.159 / 300.0 = 1.0472 : 1
Baseline at R = 1.0: Leq = 6.0' EL (theta = 30 deg)
Handbook point at R = 2.0: Leq = 28.0' EL
Interpolation: Leq = 6 + [(1.0472 - 1.0) / (2.0 - 1.0)] × (28 - 6) = 6 + 1.038 = 7.0' EL

*Prior nearest-neighbor snapping defaulted to 28' EL; the continuous engine eliminates 21' of false equivalent length.

9 Chained Schedule Forward Propagation Engine

Tool-6 enforces strict aerodynamic chaining from fan discharge to terminal diffusers. Each row automatically inherits upstream conditions:

Diverging Branch Takeoffs (5a, 5b, 5c, 6b, 7a–7c):

The critical path turns into the branch. Leaving flow cascades as branch CFM ($Q_{leaving} = Q_b$), and downstream ductwork inherits branch dimensions.

Diverging Trunk Headers (5d, 5e, 5f, 6d, 7d–7f):

The critical path stays in the main header. Leaving flow subtracts branch tap ($Q_{leaving} = Q_t - Q_b$), and downstream ductwork inherits trunk dimensions.

Cumulative Summary Metric Formulations:
• Total Duct Length: TDL = Σ Lstraight
• Total Equivalent Length: TEL = Σ Lstraight + Σ Leq, fittings
• Total Static Pressure Loss: ΔPtotal = Σ ΔPstraight + Σ ΔPfittings + Σ ΔPcomponents
• External Static Pressure: ESP = ΔPsupply critical path + ΔPreturn critical path
